## Shared visual grammar

- **Lead with a bird's-eye view.** Show the coastal form, the waterfront anchor and nearby water before exposing details. Anchor boat and jetty remain identifiable as the camera changes scale or reveals the section. Use the travel brief for angles and continuity.
- **Organize detail at three distances.** At overview scale use the shape and spacing of land, large roof groups, tree masses and the coast. At mid-scale use large material breaks such as roofs, paths, sand and seawalls. In close-ups use details that explain how the place works, such as a shaded jetty landing, mooring cleats or a survey winch. Remove fine details that produce shimmer or speckles at overview scale.
- **Model soft-edged silhouettes.** Use meaningful bevels with few segments and stable normals. Mix low-poly geometry with clean surfaces. Keep restrained contours on objects where the audit supports outlines, and test their effect at thumbnail scale. Give roofs legible eaves. Cluster vegetation with irregular species-like forms and varied heights.
- **Make real-world proportions readable.** Work in local meters, with stairs, doors and railings that align with the boat and dock. A render may simplify edge detail and compress distant background. Keep hull, deck, dock clearance and building height relationships plausible. Fill only the visible background necessary for coherent scale.
- **Break repetition intentionally.** Reuse roof, wall, vegetation and boardwalk families, then vary silhouette, orientation and spacing in groups appropriate to each shore. Use linked mesh instances for repeated assets. Vary hue sparingly within a family. Avoid a wall of identical roof modules, saturated chips scattered everywhere, or random rotations that imply crooked construction.
- **Keep the surface calm.** Water should carry a restrained surface cue, a clear shoreline edge and a legible horizon only where framing requires one. Broad roughness and luminance changes read better than full-frame high-frequency normal animation. Let the boat hull or wake supply scale. Place near-shore color transitions where they support the composition and make them optically plausible for each coast. Keep a distinct export-safe water surface.
- **A shared daylight rig with regional accents.** Start with an elevated directional key, ambient sky fill and controlled contact shadows in Eevee. Candidate first pass is sun elevation 35–50° with a diagonal shadow direction in the overview. Choose AgX or another displayed transform after comparing debug swatches and camera thumbnails. Tune exposure in the rig across the three coasts before grading individual shots. Roof and foliage detail must survive both shadow and sunlight. Treat these ranges as design seeds.
- **Leave visual room for the case.** Hold about one-third of the hero view as navigable water or uncomplicated shoreline, depending on the place. Protect an uncluttered area around the survey boat and open water beyond it. Make environment renders with text-free image files. Provide the model/section explanation alongside the render.
- **Use signed-off, portable materials.** Use predictable opaque Principled materials as the GLB baseline. Keep compositor toning, curved-layer transparency, animated ripple shaders, outline experiments and cutaway FX in authoring collections or scenes where needed. Inspect the re-imported GLB for texture, pivot and shading changes. For exporter conventions, consult the Blender manual. https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/addons/scene_gltf2.html

## Build the reusable kit around visible jobs

- Water-edge pieces: seawall edge variants, jetty supports and cap/pier modules, ladder/rail, slip or landing pieces as evidenced, quay corner, shoreline edge masks or material transitions.
- Land pieces: house forms with identifiable roof families and eave depth, doorway and shade geometry, fences or walls, clean path surfaces and village vegetation. For Amur include low reed/floodplain clusters and bank profiles. Use a separate asset gallery to demonstrate clean close-up meshes.
- Water and survey pieces: an original workboat with an identifiable profile at high distance, simpler local craft as supported, dock floats, buoys, bottle or sampler and winch where useful at mid and close range. Match the survey hardware's visible assembly to the intended depth explanation.
- Scene anchors: shared boat, jetty or Amur bank feature, section axis endpoints, approach target, coast origin and view targets. Keep transform assignments explicit so any later viewer can reposition content independently.
- Finishes: shore material edges, broad depth color, selective contact shadows, sunlight-colored highlights, local architectural detail and focused close-up texturing. Start with reusable opaque colors. Add baked textures when they improve the close-up.

## Quality and iteration

Compare all three coasts in the same gallery before granting a pass. Review each overview at ~320 px and again at full size. In grayscale, the land silhouette, landing/bank anchor and vessel should remain identifiable. Compare near and cutaway images with the boat and a fixed land landmark masked in pairs. Each place needs its own signature detail, a coherent color hierarchy, clean mesh silhouettes, and time spent on comparable final lighting. Keep at least one real before/after proof per coast with the exact change noted.

For Blender 5.2.2 on an RTX 4060 laptop with 8 GB VRAM, target fast Eevee proofs, linked vegetation and house variants, simple material slots, mesh tessellation concentrated along visible silhouettes, and 1K textures for common pieces with selective 2K textures for genuinely close items. Confirm the actual render devices and measure a representative proof with the other coastal scenes unloaded when practical. Keep enough memory headroom for Blender and the driver. Run final shots sequentially. A short Cycles/OptiX comparison is an option after checking device availability and headroom. Pick whichever renderer preserves the final static and moving view most consistently. Useful reference: https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/cycles/gpu_rendering.html
