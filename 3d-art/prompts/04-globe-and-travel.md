# DSH prompt 04 · Earth, god’s-eye travel and cutaways

Read the camera brief, globe usage and output contract. Exercise the 3D scenes, not a still-image zoom.

1. Finish the Earth scene with verified continent orientation and three geographic anchors. Use a physically lit sphere, a readable outline and space around the globe. Frame Indonesia and Primorye with appropriate globe camera rotation. Show the exact sources of the reference texture and rough anchor placement. Keep markers original and simple. Re-import the sphere export to verify texture orientation.
2. In each editable coast scene save the high-oblique overview, north-up plan, approach, section and detail camera presets. Set a usable opening/viewport view. Use named Blender timeline markers for globe region moves and native camera/view targets for each coast. List the shortcuts and marker names in the handoff. Use a lateral move at mid-distance with visible parallax across foreground, survey boat and shoreline. Render a small start/middle/end film strip so the movement can be checked without seeing full video.
3. Compose a legible globe-to-local sequence. An eased planet move, editorial scale cut and then local glide are preferred for the clear sense of scale. Hold a coastal landmark before switching to the A–B cutaway. Use comparable framing and length in all three places. Pitfall check: fixed water surface, no geometry pop, no unintended boat drift between duplicate render variants, no clipping through foreground foliage or buildings.
4. Render 540p animatics first. Inspect the contact sheet and revise camera acceleration, orientation, parallax and framing. Render final short flights and reel from resumable frame sequences. Check actual FPS, dimensions and length if an encoder is available. Keep frames and record exact missing steps when an encoder fails. Continue to 05.
