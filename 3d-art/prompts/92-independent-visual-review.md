# DSH review prompt · Use with the actual rendered images

You are reviewing a three-site Blender art delivery for The Ocean Has Floors. Read `3d-art/brief/VISUAL_DIRECTION.md`, `CAMERAS_AND_TRAVEL.md` and `OUTPUT_CONTRACT.md`. Open the actual proof PNGs, then their equal-size contact sheet. Open the flight strips or scrub MP4 playback when your harness can display it. Record which files and frames you actually saw. Ignore any author score until your own notes are written.

Evaluate each coast separately, then compare them side by side. Use the same descriptive anchors for each score. **0–3** means missing or broken, **4–6** needs a material revision, **7–8** works with a specific improvable flaw, **9–10** is consistently resolved across the set. Give an image or frame locator for every score. Leave a criterion unverified if there are no pixels available.

| Criterion | Look for |
|---|---|
| God’s-eye read | In two seconds, find land, water, the boat and the most useful orbit target. Check whether the local flight actually reveals parallax. |
| Distinct place | Find one documented landform and one credible built or natural detail per place. Hide labels briefly and try to distinguish coasts on their form. |
| Art finish | Check roof or wetland silhouettes, spacing, scale, materials, contact shadows, color variation, waterline and distracting repetition. |
| Section continuity | Point at the same physical anchors in the overhead and section. Trace the unchanged waterline. Look for clutter, bad caps and false appearance of measured geography. |
| Set coherence | Check comparable attention, readability, crop, light and film time. Point to a feature needing attention on any lagging site. |

Return a short table and at most three precise production fixes per coast. Name the screenshot region or frame where a visible problem appears and how to detect improvement. Comment separately on whether the Earth cut establishes geographic orientation. Work read-only. The production session applies fixes and records the before/after pairs.
