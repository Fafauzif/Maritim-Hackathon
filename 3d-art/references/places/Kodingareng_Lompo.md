## Model from the references

- **From orbit:** a tight, low island outline with an easily read coast. Resolve the visible split between island fill, shore transition, shallow water color and bay first. Save a north-up clay image and a high-oblique clay image before roof detailing.
- **Near the landing:** a raised pier with a readable covered end or shelter, reachable from the paths. Break some of the roof rhythm with a small waterfront landmark drawn from local forms. Use the mix of roofs and plant silhouettes to reveal the internal paths. A few outriggers near shore can establish local scale. Use original hull forms and painted color blocks.
- **For the cutaway:** keep the same recognizable pier and same survey boat in the overhead and the section. Save the boat and water surface separately. Use a restrained sand-and-rubble-looking seabed as an artistic shape. Wider land silhouettes matter more than decorative coral detail. Keep sampling objects generic and removable.

**Leave illustrative:** exact property counts and placement, the latest pier geometry, vessel identities, cross-section geometry, reef boundaries and depth layers. The exposed spit can be a scene setting informed by a documented form. It does not set a tide datum or claim the shoreline seen today.
